# Discussion draft

The HSV-2 case study illustrates why candidate selection should not collapse several
distinct questions. The leading individual guide occurs in UL36, whereas UL36 ranks 21
as a gene-level targetability portfolio. UL3 leads the exhaustive gene targetability
table, while direct HSV-2 biological evidence remains incomplete. UL30 and UL52 retain
clearer replication-focused rationales even though they do not occupy the first two
gene targetability ranks.

The new virtual analysis adds interpretable coding context without claiming repair
frequencies. Its main value is negative as well as positive: unavailable domains,
unmapped cuts, missing held-out coverage, and unreviewed evidence remain visible. The
escape calculation similarly provides a reproducible exact-target sequence barrier but
does not model mutation rates, fitness, selection, or population dynamics.

The configured panels all had a barrier equal to panel size because their targets did
not share a single disruptive substitution. This supports the narrower claim that
independent exact sites create a larger sequence-change requirement than a single
site under the declared model. It does not establish reduced biological escape.

Future work should compare the frozen panel with existing guide-design/off-target
tools, complete human evidence review, execute the same project contract on a second
virus without core-code changes, and seek independent experimental collaboration.
