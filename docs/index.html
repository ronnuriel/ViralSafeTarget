The decision gap

A good guide is not automatically a good biological target.

Most design tools optimize sequence properties. Viral research also needs population coverage, host-search boundaries, gene context, disruption hypotheses, evidence provenance, and resistance to exact-target loss.

01

Guide quality

Is the sequence targetable, conserved, and technically plausible?

02

Gene targetability

Does the gene offer a robust portfolio, not merely one exceptional guide?

03

Biological evidence

Was the role shown in this virus, an ortholog, or not yet reviewed?

04

Escape robustness

How many independent exact targets must be lost across a multiplex panel?

A central finding

Top guide ≠ top gene

UL36 contains the leading individual guide, yet ranks 21st as a gene portfolio.

One technically strong site does not establish a robust target. ViralSafeTarget keeps single-guide excellence, gene-wide targetability, and biological evidence distinct.

Evidence Agent + human review

AI can find and structure evidence. It cannot approve a biological claim.

The agent searches documented APIs, connects genes to source records, distinguishes direct-virus evidence from ortholog evidence, and prepares a review queue.

Built for reuse

Bring a different virus. Keep the same decision structure.
