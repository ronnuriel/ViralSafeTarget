# Research plan

## Phase 1 — engineering validation

- Verify coordinates and strand handling on synthetic fixtures.
- Reproduce known guide sites from published datasets.
- Add checksums and complete run manifests.
- Compare output against at least two established guide-design tools.

## Phase 2 — HSV-2 benchmark

- Freeze an accession list and reference version.
- Define quality-control rules before viewing results.
- Measure strain coverage, candidate count and host off-target burden.
- Use published HSV targets only as a held-out benchmark.
- Report sensitivity analyses across coverage and mismatch thresholds.

## Phase 3 — generalization

- Apply the same workflow to at least two additional DNA viruses.
- Test whether parameters learned or chosen on HSV transfer without manual retuning.
- Add editor profiles beyond SpCas9.

## Phase 4 — experimental collaboration

A qualified laboratory selects a small set of candidates using predefined criteria. The computational team remains blinded to experimental outcomes until predictions are frozen. Editing and antiviral measurements are then used for prospective validation.

## Publishable claims

Reasonable early claims:

- a reproducible virus-first target-prioritization framework;
- improved strain coverage or reduced host off-target burden over baselines;
- an open benchmark and provenance schema;
- prospective enrichment of experimentally active candidates.

Claims not supported by computational ranking alone:

- a cure;
- safe human use;
- elimination of latent infection;
- superiority in vivo.
