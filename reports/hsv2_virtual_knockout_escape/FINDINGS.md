# Virtual knockout and escape findings

## Computational observations

- The analyzed candidate table contained 257 unique guides.
- 81 guide-to-CDS mappings were available for the configured focus set.
- 4 configured multiplex strategies were compared on separate sequence, host-risk, disruption, and evidence axes.
- Held-out exact-target coverage was available for 200 of 257 analyzed guides; missing values remain unknown.
- UL3 leads gene-level targetability in the exhaustive source table, while direct HSV-2 biological evidence remains incomplete.
- UL30 and UL52 retain stronger replication-focused biological rationales, which are reported separately from sequence targetability.
- UL18 provides a configured capsid-assembly comparison with strong predicted disruption context in the prior case-study analysis.
- UL36 contains the leading individual guide, while the exhaustive gene-level table ranks UL36 lower as a portfolio.

## Research hypotheses

- Guides combining observed population support, interpretable coding disruption hypotheses, and independent multiplex targets may be useful candidates for independent experimental review.
- Differences between single-guide rank, gene-level targetability, and sequence escape barrier should be evaluated rather than collapsed into one therapeutic score.

## Evidence gaps

- Sequence hypotheses do not establish that an edit occurs or that a gene disruption changes viral phenotype.
- Biological evidence remains source- and virus-specific and requires explicit human review before use.
- Marginal per-guide population coverage does not establish joint panel coverage in the same genomes.

## Limitations

- Indel sizes are an equally weighted bounded grid, not biological repair probabilities.
- The escape barrier is a minimum exact-target sequence-change count, not an evolutionary probability.
- Predicted host matches are model-bounded and do not establish safety.
- No editing, viral inactivation, treatment, efficacy, delivery, or cure claim is made.
